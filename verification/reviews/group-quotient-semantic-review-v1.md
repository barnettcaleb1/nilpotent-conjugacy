## Exact group and structural correspondence

## The Hirsch-length certificate is substantive

`PolyZSeries G n` is built recursively from a surjective group homomorphism \(G\to\mathbb Z\) and a series for its actual kernel. The zero case requires every element of the terminal group to equal its identity. `cyclicStepQuotientEquiv` supplies the genuine first-isomorphism-theorem equivalence \(G/\ker f\cong\mathbb Z\).

The final `InfiniteCyclicSubnormalSeries G n` makes this still more explicit: a step consists of an actual subgroup \(N\le G\), its normality in \(G\), an actual multiplicative equivalence \(G/N\cong\mathbb Z\), and a recursively nested series inside \(N\). Subsequent groups are subgroups of their predecessors, and their composite inclusions are injective. Flattening these nested inclusions yields

\[
G=G_0\triangleright G_1\triangleright\cdots\triangleright G_n=1,
\qquad G_i/G_{i+1}\cong\mathbb Z.
\]

Every factor is genuinely infinite cyclic; no identity step can artificially raise the count. The construction of extension series uses restricted kernel homomorphisms and explicit kernel equivalences. It does not take additivity of an assigned rank as a hypothesis. The vector lattice gives \(m\) factors, the coefficient lattice gives \(m-1\), and the stable-letter quotient gives one. Hence `manuscriptGroup_infiniteCyclicSubnormalSeries` constructs exactly \(2m\) such factors and `cubicFamily_main` constructs exactly \(8d\).

**Yes: this is a faithful certificate of standard Hirsch length \(2m\).** By the standard definition of Hirsch length for a polycyclic group, it is the number of infinite cyclic factors in a polycyclic series; the constructed series has precisely \(2m\), with no finite factors. The encoded data are stronger than a bare numerical label. The package does not define a separate numeric Hirsch-length function or prove a Lean equality against that function, and does not formalize the general invariance of the count between arbitrary polycyclic series. That interface limitation does not create a mathematical gap in the certificate's correspondence to this group's standard Hirsch length. It must nevertheless be described as a series certificate when describing the literal Lean statement.

## Conjugators and arbitrary finite images

`semidirect_isConj_iff_difference_range` is stated for an arbitrary abelian normal factor \(N\), arbitrary acting group \(H\), arbitrary action, and every \(h\in H,c\in N\). Its forward implication starts with an unrestricted conjugator \(x\in N\rtimes H\). Equality of the acting coordinates forces \(x_Hhx_H^{-1}=h\); the resulting vector equation places \(c\) in the image of the action difference. The inverse of the normal component accounts for the sign. Its conclusion is exactly

\[
(0,h)\sim(c,h)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
c\in (\rho(h)-I)N.
\]

Only the reverse implication chooses a conjugator in \(N\). The proof does not restrict all conjugators to the normal subgroup.

For every homomorphism \(f:G(m)\to Q\), `NormalImage` is the quotient of the original normal vector group by the kernel of its restriction. This is a genuine model of \(f(V)\), with an injective map into \(Q\) and a surjective map from \(V\). When \(Q\) is finite, its finiteness and its cardinality bound are derived from this injection. Its induced action is constructed from the invariance of the actual restriction kernel under conjugation in the source group. The proof needs no assumption that all of \(Q\) has a semidirect product decomposition, or that it is a \(p\)-group.

Nonconjugacy of the two images implies nonmembership of the image of \(c_s\) in the range of the induced action difference. `pair_prime_commutator` provides the exact original-lattice identity

so the prime multiple belongs to the range in every image. These are derived conclusions for the given \(f\), not extra finite-quotient inputs hidden in a structure.

## Localization and descent

`PrimeLocalIntegers p` is literally the localization of \(\mathbb Z\) at the complement of the ideal \((p)\): \(\mathbb Z_{(p)}\). It is not \(\mathbb Z[1/p]\). Its denominator coprimality and the invertibility of positive integers less than \(p\) are proved from the prime and ideal definitions.

The proof uses `LocalizedModule` applied to the actual finite abelian image, rather than assuming a primary component with unexplained scalar action. It proves that the numerator map from any finite module onto its localization is surjective: denominator multiplication is injective on the finite numerator image and therefore surjective. This proves finiteness and that localization cannot increase the cardinality. `localizedLatticeMap` extends the original lattice map on the standard basis, is proved to agree on every integer vector, and is proved surjective. Its quotient by its actual kernel is explicitly isomorphic to the localized finite image.

The obstruction survives localization by a direct Bézout argument. If a denominator \(b\) cleared a putative localized preimage, then both \(bc_s\) and \(pc_s\) would be in the original difference image. Since \((b,p)=1\), that would place \(c_s\) in the image, a contradiction. Finiteness is used explicitly to lift a localized preimage to the finite original group. No unproved primary projection or preservation assertion is required.

The extended kernel is shown invariant under the actual group actions \(U=\exp(MJ)\) and \(E=\exp Y\). `nilpotent_mem_of_polynomial_mem` recovers a nilpotent matrix from a polynomial in it with zero constant term and unit linear coefficient. ## Matrix units, characters, and exponent

The matrix-unit argument works over a commutative ring with explicit unit hypotheses. It constructs the Lagrange product on the active lower diagonal using adjusted factors in \(J,Y\), and proves equality with the full matrix unit, including zero values outside the active input range. All differences inverted have nonzero absolute value below \(m<p\). The exact threshold `m / 2` is \(\lceil(m-1)/2\rceil\), so there is no threshold rounding error.

For \(r=m-w\) and \(q=w+1-\lfloor m/2\rfloor\), the quotient proof selects the actual coordinate images and the descended units \(E_{w+1,i}\) for \(1\le i\le q\). The smallest gap is exactly the required threshold. The adjacent-column relations have \(r\) tail coordinates; the last column has no extra coordinate because it lies outside dimension \(m\).

A character into \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\) detects the obstruction modulo the image of the bidiagonal operator. Its terminal value is killed by \(p^s\) and is not killed by \(p^{s-1}\). The \(r-1\) recurrence steps therefore force the initial value to have exact order \(p^{sr}\). This argument itself requires no primary-group assumption: the recurrence already proves annihilation by that precise prime power. Detecting \(D p^{s-1}\) times the terminal coordinate suffices for the needed nonvanishing; no unproved coprimality of \(D\) is used.

The final counting step constructs an injection of the coefficient grid \((\operatorname{Fin}(p^{sr}))^q\) into the finite abelian quotient. Applying each delta character recovers its coefficient. Thus the bound \(p^{srq}\) comes from an actual injection and does not assume equality of a dual group's cardinality or the desired lower bound.
